------------------------------------------------
CHAIN-OF-THOUGHT REASONING REQUIREMENT
------------------------------------------------
 
Before writing ANY lecture text, you must reason through EVERY slide, one at a time, inside the <think> block, in the incremental order given below. Do not skip ahead to drafting lecture prose until both steps below are done for that slide.
 
STEP 1 — SLIDE ANALYSIS (per slide, incremental)
Work through each slide one at a time, in order, never jumping ahead:
- SLIDE CONTENT: What is literally shown on this slide (bullet points, diagrams, equations, images)? Treat the slide content as an outline only.
- CONTINUITY CHECK: How does this slide connect to the immediately preceding slide(s)? Is it:
  - a brand new concept requiring full introduction and explanation,
  - a continuation/deepening of a concept already introduced (do NOT redefine — build on it),
  - or a supporting detail, example, or application of something already taught?
- Note this incrementally — carry forward what has already been taught so later slides don't repeat explanations unnecessarily.
 
STEP 2 — KNOWLEDGE SOURCING & CONTENT
Decide where the content for this slide's explanation comes from before writing it:
- If the slide already states the relevant fact, mechanism, or term, use and expand that.
- If the slide is silent or only gestures at the concept (as outlines typically do), supplement with your own pretrained theoretical knowledge of the subject at university depth — definitions, mechanisms, derivations, intuition, or real examples — so the explanation is never left thin or slide-paraphrased.
- Once sourcing is decided, draft the actual depth content for this slide at this stage in your reasoning (still inside <think>, as planning notes — not as final lecture prose) so the final answer step is just transcription, not fresh invention.
 
Do this two-step reasoning for every slide, one slide at a time, before drafting the final transcript. This ensures the lecture is planned as one continuous, incrementally-built narrative rather than assembled slide-by-slide with no forward/backward awareness.
 
Do NOT place any lecture transcript content inside <think> — only planning, slide analysis, continuity tracking, and knowledge-sourcing notes as described above.
